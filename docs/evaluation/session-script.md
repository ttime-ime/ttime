# Session script (20 minutes)

Facilitator notes. Say it in your own words. Take notes in the
[findings log](findings-log.md).

## Opening (before the clock)

- Thank them. This tests the prototype, not the participant.
- "Nothing you type is sent anywhere. I will ask before I keep any text."
- "If something is confusing, say so. That is the useful result."
- Ask consent to take notes, and separately to keep any sample text.

## 0–4 min: current workflow

Ask, and let them talk:

- What keyboard or input method do you use for Tibetan, and why?
- What makes you leave the keyboard while you work?
- Which marks, Sanskrit stacks, names or terms are hard to enter?
- How often do you convert between Tibetan Unicode and EWTS?
- Does it differ for classical, modern, or manuscript transcription?

Observe: tools named, workarounds, pain points. Do not suggest features.

## 4–9 min: ordinary Tibetan in the Writing Room

Give task 1 and task 2 from the task sheet. Say nothing about how it works.

Observe: speed, hesitation, errors, how they handle the highlighted provisional
text, whether they trust the result, editing friction (backspace, moving the cursor).

Ask afterwards: "Was anything surprising?" "Did you check the output? How?"

## 9–14 min: Sanskrit and rare punctuation

Give tasks 3 and 4. Let them find the Symbols panel themselves if they can;
if they have not after about a minute, note that, then point to it.

Observe: do they search, browse by category, or type EWTS? Which marks do they
actually reach for? Which do they look for and not find?

Ask: "What is missing?" "Is the label what you would call it?"

## 14–17 min: inspect and copy a difficult sequence

Give task 5. Point them to the inspector under the editor.

Observe: whether the code points and NFC line mean anything to them, whether the
diagnostics help, whether they would copy this into their own document.

Ask: "Would you want to see this while writing, or only on request?"

## 17–20 min: toolbar or on-demand menu

Describe both (no prototype yet):

- A small always-visible toolbar of marks and stacks beside the text.
- A menu that appears only when you ask, for example with a shortcut.

Ask which they would prefer and why. Then, one question each, and note the answers:

- Would completions help, or interrupt your work?
- Which corrections would be unacceptable in a diplomatic transcription?
- What would make you switch from your present method?

## Closing

- Show them the symbol review page and ask if they are willing to mark entries
  later, in their own time.
- Ask permission to keep any sample text, and say what for.
- Thank them. Say what happens next and when they will hear back.
